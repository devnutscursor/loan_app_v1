MLO DATA 
This tab is the MLO Data section. "MLO" stands for Mortgage Loan Originator. In plain terms, 
this is the salesperson or loan officer who actually spoke to the borrower and took the 
application. 
For a developer, this page is essentially a GROUP BY MLO_ID query. It takes the total volume 
of your company and breaks it down by which specific employee brought the files in. 
Here is the breakdown of what these data points mean for your software: 
1. The "Who": Rabi Uddin (NMLS: 123456) 
In the mortgage industry, loan officers are heavily regulated. They cannot simply be "Sales Rep 
#5"; they must hold a federal/state license. 
● NMLS ID (123456): This is the ultimate Primary Key for a mortgage professional. The 
Nationwide Multistate Licensing System (NMLS) assigns this unique 6-digit number to an 
individual for life. 
● Developer Logic: Your Loan_Files table must have a foreign key linking to a Users 
table, and that Users table must require a valid NMLS ID. If a loan file doesn't have an 
MLO's NMLS ID attached to it, the MCR export will fail. 
2. The Math (Amount, Count, Average) 
If you remember the very first "Application Data" screen we looked at, the company took in 
exactly 3 applications totaling $1,080,000. 
Because Rabi Uddin is the only MLO listed here, it means his ID was attached to 100% of the 
company's volume for this quarter. 
● Count (3): He originated 3 files. 
● Amount ($1,080,000): The total dollar sum of those 3 files. 
● Average ($360,000): $1,080,000 / 3. 
3. Why Regulators Require This (The "Bad Apple" Rule) 
You might wonder why the NMLS cares about individual employee performance if they are 
already tracking the company's total volume. 
They use this specific data block to track individuals across the entire industry. If Rabi Uddin 
writes terrible, high-risk loans and gets fired, he takes his NMLS ID to his next company. 
Regulators use this data to flag "bad actors" who jump from company to company originating 
toxic or fraudulent loans.
