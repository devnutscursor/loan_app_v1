RMLA stands for the Residential Mortgage Lending Act. While the previous tabs were about 
tracking money and loan types, this tab is a Risk Profile of your entire operation. Regulators 
use this screen to see if the software is pumping out safe, standard loans, or highly toxic, risky 
ones. 
For a developer, this page is a series of data matrix queries. It takes the same two funded loans 
($720,000 total) and groups them by risk factors. Let's break it down block by block. 
1. Residential First Mortgages (T010 - T100) 
This section only cares about standard, primary mortgages (1st Liens on 1-4 unit houses - 
SFR). It categorizes them using a 3-dimensional matrix: Government vs. Conventional, Fixed 
vs. ARM, and Conforming vs. Jumbo. 
● Government vs. Conventional: Is it backed by the feds (FHA/VA) or private money? 
● Fixed vs. ARM: Is the rate static, or does it adjust? 
● Conforming vs. Non-Conforming (Jumbo): "Conforming" means the loan amount is 
small enough to fit within Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac guidelines. "Jumbo" means it's a loan 
that exceeds federal limits. 
● Dev Logic: Your query here would look like: WHERE Lien_Position == '1st' AND 
Property_Type == '1-4 Unit' GROUP BY (Loan_Type, Amortization_Type, 
Loan_Size_Limit). 
2. Other Mortgages (T110 - T200) 
This is the "Everything Else" bucket. If a loan didn't fit into the standard residential box above, it 
lands here. 
● T110/T120 (Seconds & HELOCs): Subordinate loans (2nd in line to get paid). 
● T130 (Reverse Mortgages): Paying seniors out of their equity. 
● T140 - T170 (Construction & Commercial): Loans to build a house from scratch, or 
loans for businesses/apartment complexes. 
The "Second in Line" Loans 
These are loans where another bank has the primary claim to the house. 
● T110 (Closed-End Second Mortgages): A one-time lump sum loan. The borrower gets 
$50k at closing and pays it back over a set term. 
○ Dev Logic: Lien_Position == '2nd' AND Revolving == False. 
● T120 (HELOCs): A Home Equity Line of Credit. This is essentially a credit card tied to 
the house. The borrower is approved for $100k, but might only take $10k at closing. 
○ Dev Logic: Lien_Position == '2nd' (usually) AND Revolving == True. 
○ CRITICAL COMPLIANCE BUG: Regulators explicitly note to "include the credit 
line amount." Do not report the amount the borrower drew at closing; your SQL 
query must pull the Maximum_Credit_Limit field. 
The Reverse Mortgage 
● T130 (Reverse Mortgages): A loan exclusively for seniors where the bank pays the 
borrower out of their equity, and the loan is repaid when the borrower dies or moves. 
○ Dev Logic: This is a checksum tie-back. The total here must perfectly match the 
AC790 (Total Reverse Mortgages) from the "Closed Loan Data" tab. 
The Construction Bucket (Dirt to Roof) 
Construction loans are fundamentally different in software because they aren't fully funded on 
day one. They are paid out in "Draws" as the builder finishes stages of the house. 
● T140 (Construction, 1-4 Unit Residential): Building a standard house or duplex. 
○ Dev Logic: Loan_Purpose == 'Construction' AND Property_Type == '1-4 Unit'. 
● T150 (Construction, 5+ Unit Residential): Building an apartment complex or a massive 
condo building. 
○ Dev Logic: Loan_Purpose == 'Construction' AND Property_Type == 
'Multi-Family'. 
● T160 (Construction, Commercial): Building a strip mall, warehouse, or office building. 
○ Dev Logic: Loan_Purpose == 'Construction' AND Property_Type == 
'Commercial'. 
The Commercial & Alternative Bucket 
● T170 (Commercial Mortgage): A standard, closed loan for a business property (like 
refinancing an existing retail plaza). 
○ Developer Warning: Commercial loans are exempt from most standard 
consumer protection laws (like TRID or RESPA). If the system handles these (it 
will), the UI should likely hide the standard "Consumer Disclosure" tracking fields 
when this property type is selected to prevent user confusion. 
● T180 (Land Contract): Also known as a "Contract for Deed." The seller acts as the 
bank, and the buyer doesn't get the legal title to the property until the final payment is 
made years later. 
○ Dev Logic: Usually tracked via a specific Financing_Type == 'Land Contract' 
enum. 
● T200 (Total Other Mortgages): The sum of T110 through T180.

3. Origination Channel (T210 - T240) 
This tracks how the loan was generated and funded. 
● T210 (Brokered): You were the middleman (1 loan for $360k). 
● T220 (Closed - Retail): You dealt directly with the consumer and funded the loan under 
your own name (1 loan for $360k). Note: In many systems, "Non-Delegated 
Correspondent" volume rolls up into Retail for this specific section because you dealt 
directly with the borrower. 
4. Risk Characteristics (T250 - T340) 
These are the "Red Flags." Regulators scrutinize this heavily to ensure another 2008 housing 
crash doesn't happen. 
● Alt / Reduced Documentation: Loans where you didn't verify standard W-2s or tax 
returns (e.g., using bank statements). 
● Interest Only / Option ARM: Risky loan types where the borrower isn't paying down the 
actual principal balance. 
● Prepayment Penalty: Charging the borrower a fee for paying off the loan too early. 
● Mortgage Insurance (T310): Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) is required when a 
borrower puts down less than 20%.  
● Dev Logic: These are almost entirely driven by Boolean checkboxes in your database. 
5. LTV Distribution (T370 - T430) 
LTV stands for Loan-to-Value. It measures how much debt the borrower has compared to the 
actual appraised value of the house. 
● The Math: LTV = (Loan Amount / Appraised Value) * 100 
● The Buckets: The regulators want to see these LTVs grouped into ranges. 
● In your screenshot: Both loans ($720k total) fell into the ≤ 60% bucket. This means the 
borrowers put down massive down payments (40% or more), making these extremely 
safe loans for the bank. 
6. Weighted Averages & Pull-Through 
This is the final analytics dashboard for the quarter. 
● Pull-Through Ratio (66.67%): This is your conversion rate. (Loans Funded / 
Applications Received) * 100. You took in 3 applications and funded 2, giving you 
66.67%. 
● Weighted Averages: Instead of a simple average, these fields average out the LTV and 
Coupon Rate (Interest Rate), but weighted by the size of the loan. A $1 million loan 
impacts this average much more than a $100k loan. 
● Dev Note: The Weighted Average LTV is currently showing 0% in your UI. This looks 
like a bug in the code! If both loans are in the ≤ 60% bucket, the weighted average 
cannot be 0%. Your calculation script might be missing a data point.
